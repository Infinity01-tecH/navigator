# Marketing Website Handoff — "Navigator"

## Context

You asked for a marketing website plan/handoff for the app — the content, pages, features to
showcase, CTAs, and marketing messaging — **with no design direction**. This document is the
handoff. It intentionally says nothing about visual design, layout, colors, or components; it is a
brief for *what the marketing site must communicate and how it should behave as an experience*,
which a designer/copywriter/marketer can take and design freely.

**One important correction from studying the codebase.** The product is not a "language learning
app." It is **Navigator**, an **AI-powered personal learning-roadmap generator** that can teach
*any* skill or subject. What was described as "vocabulary cards / a Vocabulary tab" is actually a
*visual card style* used to render roadmap phases and steps — there is no vocabulary feature. The
real app has **five tabs: Today, Inspire, Roadmap, Chat, Profile**, plus onboarding, an Immersive
video mode, notes, and a social/accountability layer. The marketing site should represent the real
product. If the intent really is to position this narrowly as a *language* learning tool, flag it —
that would be a repositioning, not a handoff of the current product.

---

## 1. What we are marketing (product truth)

Navigator turns "I want to learn X" into a complete, personalized, day-by-day plan built from
**real, verified resources** — then keeps the learner going with streaks, XP, quizzes,
achievements, an AI tutor, and accountability partners.

**One-line positioning (working):** *"Tell Navigator what you want to learn. Get a personalized
roadmap of real videos, articles, tools, and experts in 3 minutes — then actually finish it."*

**Core differentiators to hammer throughout the site:**
- **Personalized, not generic.** A learning-fingerprint quiz + self-rated level + an AI calibration
  quiz + your available time all shape the plan. Advanced learners skip the beginner filler.
- **Real content, never hallucinated.** The generator has a strict rule against inventing links; it
  resolves to real videos/articles or it doesn't ship the link. This is a trust story — market it.
- **Built to finish, not just to start.** Streaks (with freezes + grace days), XP/levels, quiz
  gates, achievements, a projected finish date, and accountability partners fight the "I signed up
  and quit" problem every learner knows.
- **Learns anything.** Skills (React, Python) or academic subjects (Computer Science, Biology).
- **Three ways to build a plan:** Full Roadmap, Sprint Blueprint (1–4 weeks, goal-driven),
  YouTube-Based (built around creators you already trust).

## 2. Target audiences (who the copy speaks to)

1. **Self-directed learners drowning in options** — "there are 10,000 tutorials, which do I do and
   in what order?" Navigator answers the ordering/curation problem.
2. **Career switchers / skill-uppers** — motivated by outcomes and job-market relevance (the app
   already tracks salary insights and job-demand trend data behind the scenes).
3. **Serial course-abandoners** — people who start and quit; sell the retention/accountability system.
4. **YouTube-first learners** — people who already learn from creators and want structure on top.

Keep one primary audience per page section; don't blend messages.

## 3. Site map (pages) & purpose

**Primary pages**
1. **Home / Landing** — the full pitch in one scroll. (Details in §4.)
2. **How It Works** — the 3-minute journey: name a skill → personalize → get roadmap → learn daily.
3. **Features** — deep-dive on each pillar (Roadmaps, Today/daily plan, Inspire feed, Navi AI chat,
   Immersive Mode, Notes, Streaks & XP, Achievements, Accountability partners).
4. **Modes** — dedicated explainer for Full Roadmap vs Sprint Blueprint vs YouTube-Based (this is a
   genuinely differentiated feature worth its own page).
5. **Pricing** — tiers/CTA (placeholder until monetization is decided; see §8 open questions).
6. **Use Cases / For…** — e.g. "For career switchers," "For developers," "For students." Each is a
   tailored landing page reusing the same product, different framing.
7. **FAQ** — trust, "is the content real," data/privacy, accounts, cost.
8. **About** — mission + the "why we built this" story (curation & completion problem).

**Secondary / supporting**
- **Blog / Learning Hub** — SEO engine; long-tail "how to learn X" content that funnels into "let
  Navigator build the X roadmap." Huge organic-search opportunity given the app is subject-agnostic.
- **Changelog / What's New** — shows momentum.
- **Legal**: Privacy Policy, Terms, Cookie/consent.
- **Contact / Support**.
- **404** — on-brand, routes back to "start a roadmap."

**Utility (no marketing content, just links):** login / sign-up (deep-links into the app),
social profile links.

## 4. Home page — content blocks (order = narrative, not layout)

Each block below is a *message*, not a design. Sequence them so the story builds.

1. **Hero** — the promise + primary CTA. Message: name anything, get a real plan in 3 minutes.
   Include the skill/subject input idea as the emotional hook ("What do you want to learn?").
2. **Social proof strip** — logos/press/user counts/ratings when available. (Placeholder now.)
3. **The problem** — "You don't lack tutorials. You lack a path, and a reason to keep going."
   Name the pain: too many options, no order, no accountability, courses abandoned.
4. **How it works (3 steps)** — name a skill → 3-minute personalization → daily plan you follow.
5. **The roadmap payoff** — what a generated roadmap actually contains: phases → steps
   (Watch/Read/Build/Practice), real tools you'll use, real experts to follow, milestones,
   even salary ranges and adjacent "what to learn next" skills.
6. **"Real content" trust block** — no fake links, no hallucinated resources. Verified videos,
   articles, tools, and creators.
7. **Retention system** — streaks, XP & levels, quiz gates, achievements, projected finish date.
   Message: "Most apps get you started. Navigator gets you finished."
8. **Three modes** — Full / Sprint / YouTube-based, one sentence each, link to Modes page.
9. **The AI tutor (Navi)** — ask anything, get answers tied to what you're learning; save to notes.
10. **Immersive Mode** — turn any YouTube video into a guided, chaptered lesson with knowledge checks.
11. **Accountability & social** — friends, streak partners who see your progress, reactions.
12. **Outcome/aspiration block** — restate the transformation ("from scattered to shipped").
13. **Final CTA** — repeat the hero CTA; remove all friction here.
14. **Footer** — full nav, legal, social.

## 5. Feature messaging bank (what to say per feature)

Use these as the "things to say." One benefit-led headline + a plain explanation each.

- **AI Roadmap Generator** — "A complete plan, built for you, in 3 minutes." Phases, steps, real
  resources, tools, experts, milestones, salary insight, and next-skill suggestions.
- **Personalization engine** — "It learns how *you* learn." Fingerprint quiz + level check + time
  budget shape difficulty, pacing, and content.
- **Today (daily plan)** — "Open the app, know exactly what to do today." A pinned set of daily
  tasks with resources one tap away; progress toward a daily XP goal.
- **Step types (Watch/Read/Build/Practice)** — "Learn, then prove it." Watch steps require passing a
  quick quiz to complete — no passive box-ticking.
- **Inspire feed** — "A short-video feed that actually moves you forward." Discovery content tied to
  what you're learning, not doom-scroll.
- **Navi (AI chat tutor)** — "A tutor on call." Voice input, multiple conversations, save answers to
  notes tied to a topic.
- **Immersive Mode** — "Any YouTube video becomes a real lesson." AI chapters, knowledge checks,
  capture-as-you-watch notes, XP.
- **Notes Hub** — "One place for everything you learn." Skill folders, per-topic notes, rich editor.
- **Streaks & XP / Levels** — "Momentum you can see." Streak freezes and grace days so one bad day
  doesn't wipe your progress; 50 XP/step, level up every 1000 XP.
- **Achievements** — "Milestones worth chasing." Tiered badges (Bronze→Legendary), shareable.
- **Accountability partners & friends** — "Learn with someone." Streak partners see your daily plan;
  send requests, react, keep each other honest.
- **Finish-date projection** — "Know when you'll actually be done." Estimates completion from your
  real pace.
- **Job-market relevance** — "Learn what's in demand." Salary ranges + demand/trend signals behind
  skill suggestions. (Powered by real job/dev/interest data — market as insight, don't over-promise.)
- **Skill Crossover** — "Where this skill takes you next." AI-suggested adjacent skills you can turn
  into a new roadmap in one tap.

## 6. CTAs (the actions we want)

- **Primary CTA (everywhere):** *"Build my roadmap"* / *"Start learning free"* — deep-links into the
  in-app Start screen so the user experiences the 3-minute magic immediately. This is the single most
  important conversion path; the product *is* its own best demo.
- **Secondary CTA:** *"See how it works"* (scroll/anchor or How It Works page) for the not-ready-yet.
- **Tertiary / low-commitment:** *"Try Immersive Mode — paste a YouTube link"* as a zero-signup taste
  of the product (the app supports guest/preview roadmaps before sign-in — lean on this to reduce
  signup friction).
- **Content CTA (blog/hub):** each article ends with "Let Navigator build the [topic] roadmap."
- **Newsletter/waitlist CTA** (if pre-launch or for the blog).

Rule for the marketer: **every page ends in a CTA**, and the primary CTA copy stays consistent
sitewide so it's unmistakable.

## 7. UX principles for the marketing site (behavior, not visuals)

- **Show the product doing the work, not adjectives.** The wow is a real generated roadmap; let the
  demo/CTA carry it. Favor an interactive or live "type a skill → preview" moment over static claims.
- **Progressive disclosure.** Home tells the story at a glance; Features/Modes/Use-Case pages hold the
  depth. Don't dump everything on the landing page.
- **One message per section, one CTA intent per page.** Avoid competing asks.
- **Frictionless path to value.** The primary CTA should reach the "name a skill" moment in as few
  clicks as possible; exploit guest/preview so people can try before creating an account.
- **Trust signals near claims.** The "real, verified content" and privacy claims should sit next to
  the features they justify, not buried in a footer.
- **Consistency of language.** Lock a glossary: Roadmap, Phase, Step, Streak, XP, Level, Navi,
  Immersive Mode, Streak Partner, Skill Crossover. Use these exact terms everywhere so the site and
  the app speak the same language.
- **Mobile-first behavior.** The product is a mobile-first app; assume most visitors arrive on
  phones. Structure content and CTAs for that reading pattern.
- **Fast + accessible.** Performance and accessibility are UX, not polish — required, not optional.
- **SEO-driven information architecture.** Use-case pages + the learning-hub blog are the growth
  engine; structure URLs and content around "learn [skill]" intent.

---

## 8. Copy tone & message guardrails

- **Tone:** motivating, direct, credible. Confident but not hypey. Talks *to* a learner, not down.
- **Say:** personalized, real/verified resources, finish what you start, learn anything, in demand.
- **Don't say / avoid over-claiming:** guaranteed jobs, guaranteed salaries, "the only app you need,"
  or specific outcome guarantees. Frame salary/demand as *insight*, not a promise.
- **Trust must be earned in copy:** when claiming "real content" or "personalized," immediately show
  the mechanism (the URL policy, the fingerprint/level/time inputs). Proof beats adjectives.

## 9. Open questions to resolve before writing final copy

These need answers from you; they change the messaging materially:

1. **Positioning:** general "learn anything" (matches the product) vs a narrow "language learning"
   angle (does not match current features). Which is it?
2. **Pricing/monetization:** free, freemium, paid tiers? Determines the Pricing page and CTA copy.
3. **Launch stage:** live, beta, or waitlist? Changes CTAs (sign up vs join waitlist) and social proof.
4. **Product name:** confirm "Navigator" is the public brand name for marketing.
5. **Platform reality:** is there a public web app / app-store presence to link to, or is this
   currently the Figma Make preview only? Affects the primary CTA destination.
6. **Available proof assets:** any user counts, testimonials, press, ratings to feature?
